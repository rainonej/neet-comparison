The leaked paper exposed an obvious injustice. This page asks a harder question: what if nothing had leaked? What if every student received exactly the same paper under honest supervision?

Would NEET then be fair?

No. Scroll to see why.

01 · The promise

One paper. One rank.
Everyone gets a fair shot.

That is the seduction. No interviews. No family connections on the form. The same questions for every candidate. A single list that looks like pure measurement.

  • One national exam
  • Identical questions
  • One rank list
  • Merit decides

We use the reconstructed 2024 national marks distribution because it lets us study NEET’s ordinary structure — independently of the 2026 leak. This is not an analysis of a corrupted year.

02 · The prize

Medicine is worth wanting.

Families are not irrational. An MBBS seat still offers income, professional security, social respect, and a path into a more stable future than many alternatives.

Why people keep returning

  • Security and structured careers in health labor markets
  • Status families treat as destiny, not a hobby
  • Earnings that still outrun most non-professional paths

Modeled profession medians put medicine near ₹4.5–4.8L against roughly ₹3.3L for engineering and under ₹1L for no college — before counting security and prestige. The prize is real. That is why the rationing hurts.

Profession earnings are illustrative employed-path medians from the project wage model, not a promise of any individual’s outcome. See evidence lab.

03 · Guaranteed disappointment

The system requires
most people to lose.

In 2024, about 2.33 million candidates appeared for roughly 129,600 MBBS seats — about 18 candidates for every seat.

“Qualified” is not access. The word promises far more than the seats can deliver.

04 · The knife edge

A four-mark answer can rearrange a life
without measuring an enormous difference.

Near the capacity line, thousands of candidates sit within a few correct answers of each other. Preparation matters. Knowledge matters. But among people packed this tightly, a guess, a fever, a familiar drill question, or one blank can still decide who gets a future.

05 · Unequal tickets

Before the exam measures the student,
the system has already measured the family.

Two candidates can receive the identical paper. Their runways into that paper are not identical. Language of schooling, coaching markets, the ability to relocate, the ability to repeat a year, and whether a private seat offer can be cashed — all of that is already in play.

Shorter runway

  • Less advantaged school medium
  • Limited or no paid preparation
  • Harder to move for coaching
  • Cannot afford to repeat years
  • Private offer is unusable without money

Longer runway

  • Advantaged medium & exam vernacular
  • Intensive paid preparation
  • Metro coaching markets within reach
  • Family can fund drop years
  • Can cash a private seat if ranked into one

Same paper on exam day · Different ticket to get there

Human anchors reserved — a repeater, a family paying for preparation, someone who qualified but could not cash a private offer. Real accounts forthcoming.

06 · The laundering

Unequal lives go in.
Merit comes out.

The rank is a real measurement of where someone finished after unequal preparation, time, language access, geography, and fallback options. The lie is what society does next: erase those inputs and attribute the number to the child alone.

07 · Pay to stand still

Everyone runs harder.
The seats do not move.

Coaching can help one student. That is why families buy it. Once nearly everyone who can pay also buys it, opting out becomes dangerous — but the number of seats has not increased. Families pay more merely to avoid falling behind.

Beat 1 — One family buys coaching

One student rises slightly in the crowd. The purchase looks rational. It may be.

Beat 2 — Everyone who can afford it responds

More platforms appear. Absolute scores may rise. Relative ranks barely change. The seat count stays fixed.

Beat 3 — Unequal endurance

Some families buy another year, a residential program, a taller platform. Others step off when money or time runs out.

Coaching does not need to be useless for the system to be wasteful.
It only needs to become necessary for standing still.

This is the arms-race mechanism in plain language. The project encodes it as a sensitivity explainer with skeptical priors — not a national causal coaching ROI. Parameter sweeps live in the evidence lab.

08 · Years as currency

The system rewards those who can survive
another year inside it.

In Tamil Nadu’s MBBS admitted cohorts, repeaters rose from about 12.5% in 2016–17 to 71.4% in 2020–21. Within four years, the typical admitted cohort flipped from mostly current-year students to overwhelmingly students who had already left school and returned to try again.

What percentage of 2020–21 TN admits were current-year students?
About 28.6%. The rest had already paid in years.

The leaked paper broke the rules.
The ordinary system reveals what the rules are for.

Millions pay in money, years, and fear for a scarce prize. Privilege buys better odds. The rank list hides the price of the ticket. Then inequality returns wearing the language of merit.

A taxing lottery sold as a fair race.
Scarce seats. Unequal tickets. A rank that launders the difference.
