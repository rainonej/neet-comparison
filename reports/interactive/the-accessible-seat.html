A fair paper is not a fair race.

The leaked paper exposed an obvious injustice. This essay asks the harder question: what if nothing had leaked, every candidate received the same paper, and every answer was marked correctly?

Would NEET then be fair?

No. Scroll to see why ↓

01 · The promise

One paper. One rank. Everyone gets a fair shot.

That is the seduction: no interview, no family name on the form, the same questions for every candidate, and a single list that looks like pure measurement.

One national exam
Identical questions
One rank list
Merit decides

The analysis uses the reconstructed 2024 national marks distribution so the ordinary system can be examined independently of the 2026 leak. This is not an analysis of a corrupted exam year.

02 · The prize

Medicine is worth wanting. That is what makes the trap work.

An MBBS seat can mean a structured profession, social respect, financial security, and upward mobility for an entire family. People return because the prize is real—not because they are foolish.

The promise to the studentA respected profession and a stable future.
The promise to the familyA generation-changing return on sacrifice.

03 · Guaranteed disappointment

The system requires most candidates to lose.

In 2024, about 2.33 million candidates appeared for roughly 129,602 MBBS seats—about 18 candidates for every seat.

“Qualified” is not access. The word promises far more than the seats can deliver.

18 candidates. One chair.

The exam does not discover that seventeen students are unworthy. It begins with only one place for them.

04 · The knife edge

This was a real NEET question. A guess can create a five-mark gap.

The item below is ordinary, valid syllabus material—not a trick question. It can test whether someone recalls an inorganic-chemistry fact. But the consequence attached to that fact is much larger than the difference it observes between two future doctors.

Official NEET-UG 2020English booklet E1 · Q101 · Chemistry

Which of the following oxoacid of sulphur has an –O–O– linkage?

Choose an answer. Or ask the page to make a blind guess.

Correct: +4. Wrong: −1. Blank: 0. A blind four-option guess has an expected value of +0.25 marks.

Correct answer: C. Peroxodisulphuric acid contains the peroxide –O–O– linkage.

This is real chemistry knowledge. The item does not directly observe clinical judgment, communication, ethics, reliability, uncertainty management or care.

05 · Unequal tickets

Before the exam measures the student, the system has already measured the family.

Two candidates can receive the same paper. Their runways into that paper are not the same.

Shorter runway

  • School language less aligned with exam resources
  • Limited paid test-specific preparation
  • Harder to relocate to a coaching market
  • Cannot finance repeated drop years
  • A private offer is unusable without money

Longer runway

  • School language aligned with exam vernacular
  • Intensive paid preparation
  • Coaching hubs and residential programs within reach
  • Family can finance another attempt
  • Private college remains a purchasable fallback

Same paper on exam day · Different ticket to reach it

06 · The laundering

Unequal lives go in. Merit comes out.

The rank is real: it records where someone finished after unequal preparation, time, language access, geography and fallback options. The lie begins when those inputs are erased and the number is attributed to the child alone.

07 · Pay to stand still

Everyone runs harder. The seats do not move.

Coaching can help one student. That is why families buy it. When everyone who can pay responds, preparation becomes the entry fee rather than a lasting advantage. Opting out becomes dangerous, while capacity stays fixed.

One family buys
1 seat
Everyone responds
1 seat
Money runs out
1 seat

Coaching does not need to be useless for the system to be wasteful. It only needs to become necessary for standing still.

08 · Years as currency

The system rewards those who can survive another year inside it.

Among Tamil Nadu’s admitted MBBS cohorts, the repeater share rose from about 12.5% in 2016–17 to 71.4% in 2020–21. Within four years, the admitted cohort flipped from mostly current-year students to overwhelmingly people who had already left school and returned to try again.

Only 28.6% of the 2020–21 admitted cohort were current-year students. The rest had already paid in time.

The leak broke the rules. The ordinary system shows why clean rules are not enough.

Millions pay in money, years and fear for a deliberately scarce prize. Privilege buys a longer runway and more chances to remain in the race. The rank list hides the price of the ticket. Then inequality returns wearing the language of merit.

A fair paper cannot make an unfair race fair.
