# Researcher and public-data access findings

## Bottom line

No lawful public national file was identified that links NEET applicant socioeconomic background, coaching, score, admission and later outcomes. However, the search substantially improved the acquisition plan. One openly described aspirant microdataset exists, several research teams hold relevant respondent-level files, Tamil Nadu has a traceable administrative data chain, and Kerala provides a strong public admissions adapter.

## Highest-value discoveries

1. The 2026 Biswas–Maji study provides an anonymized NEET/JEE aspirant dataset and codebook through OSF.
2. The A.K. Rajan Committee relied on data supplied by identifiable Tamil Nadu administrative bodies, including a detailed 2,583-student government/aided-school cohort.
3. Alagappa University researchers surveyed 400 NEET-oriented students with coaching intensity and repeat-attempt variables.
4. A Puducherry medical-college team linked NEET scores to first-year MBBS outcomes for 150 admitted students.
5. A Guntur team surveyed test anxiety and demographics among 200 NEET aspirants.
6. A Kerala study contains social-origin and programme-choice data for 523 backward-class students across medicine and alternative courses.
7. Kerala CEE, NMC aggregate extraction and NIRF medical filings can supply large public validation panels without waiting for NTA.
8. Historical Ministry of Education RTI requests provide exact keys for locating or requesting archived replies on financial status, coaching expenditure and candidate categories.

## Negative result

English, Tamil, Malayalam and Hindi searches did not reveal a credible public mirror of the national NTA applicant database with income, parental education, coaching and score. Forum posts and coaching sites yielded lived-experience material and document leads but not defensible probability data.

## Recommended next move

Download the OSF and OpenICPSR packages first, then send narrow author requests. In parallel, build the Kerala admissions and NIRF panels. Treat the Tamil Nadu committee route as targeted scholarly outreach rather than a generic request to the current central government.
